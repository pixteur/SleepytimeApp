THE OBSIDIAN STONE


Chapter One: The Door in the Silver Tree

Bob and Leo stood beneath the oldest tree in the Luminous Forest.

Its trunk was wider than a house. Its silver bark shimmered in the moonlight, and its great roots curled across the ground like sleeping dragons.

Bob placed one hand against the trunk. The bark felt warm.

“This tree is glowing from the inside,” he said.

Leo rolled closer on his quiet wheels. A blue light moved across the robot’s single round eye as he scanned the tree.

“I have detected an unusual source of energy,” Leo said. “It appears to be hidden inside the trunk.”

Bob smiled. He loved hidden things, especially when they involved ancient trees, strange energy, or secret machines.

“So the tree is keeping a secret,” he said.

“That is not the most scientific explanation,” Leo replied.

“But it is the most exciting one.”

Leo thought for a moment.

“Agreed.”

The bark began to shine beneath Bob’s hand.

He stepped back as a smooth section of the trunk flowed aside. It did not crack or creak. It moved like a silver curtain.

Behind it stood a tall, arched doorway.

A passage curved down into the tree. Soft golden light pulsed far below, steady and slow. It looked like a lantern glowing beneath a blanket.

Bob leaned forward. A faint melody drifted through the passage. It sounded like leaves moving in a summer breeze, even though the air was perfectly still.

“A secret doorway inside an ancient tree,” Bob whispered. “We have to see where it goes.”

“There is a small amount of uncertainty,” Leo said.

“Is that robot language for yes?”

“It is robot language for stay close.”

Together, they crossed the silver doorway.

The floor felt soft beneath Bob’s shoes, although it looked like polished wood. Threads of light ran through the walls. They twisted and branched like roots, rivers, and the circuits inside one of Bob’s inventions.

The passage spiraled gently downward. With every step, the quiet melody grew clearer.

Bob did not know what waited for them below. Yet Leo rolled beside him, and the warm light showed them the way.

It felt as if the ancient tree had been waiting for them.


Chapter Two: The Heart of the Forest

The passage opened into an enormous round chamber.

Its ceiling rose so high that it vanished into a cloud of golden mist. In the center stood a great crystal tower. It looked as though someone had built it from hundreds of clear, glowing blocks and then allowed the forest to grow around it.

Tiny lights moved inside the crystal like slow stars.

Bob walked closer.

Curving symbols covered its surface. Some looked like leaves and flowing water. Others looked like plans for a wonderful and very complicated machine.

“It is a little like a giant computer,” Bob said.

“A computer made from crystal and living wood,” Leo replied. “I approve of the design.”

Near the bottom of the tower, Bob noticed a small blue mark. It was shaped like a fox’s paw.

“Do you think this is a button?” he asked.

“Possibly.”

“A magical button?”

“My operating system does not include that category.”

“You need an update.”

Bob placed his palm over the paw print.

Warmth spread through his fingers.

The crystal gave a clear and beautiful chime. Golden light rushed into the walls. It traveled upward through the tree in thousands of shining threads.

Then pictures appeared inside the crystal.

Bob saw silver leaves swaying beneath the moon. He saw a family of amber-eyed foxes padding through a fern-filled glade. He saw a bright stream winding between carpets of glowing moss. Fireflies rose above a quiet pool and turned in a slow circle like a tiny galaxy.

“It is showing us the forest,” Bob said.

Leo studied the changing pictures.

“I believe this chamber is connected to every part of the Luminous Forest,” he said. “The crystal receives information from the trees, streams, plants, and animals.”

“So it is a map.”

“Yes. It may also help the forest share its energy.”

The pictures shifted.

At the edge of the glowing map, one patch of woodland had faded to a pale gray. Its trees drooped. Its fireflies moved slowly. The whole place seemed tired.

The crystal gave another chime.

This note was soft and low. It sounded almost like a sigh.

Bob’s smile faded.

“Something is wrong there.”

A silver line appeared inside the crystal. It traced a path from the ancient tree to the fading grove.

“The crystal is showing us the way,” Bob said.

Leo’s blue eye brightened.

“Then we should investigate.”


Chapter Three: The Whispering Willow Grove

Bob and Leo followed the silver path out of the tree and into the forest.

A little arrow of light floated above the trail. It stayed a few steps ahead of them, moving silently between the ferns.

At first, the Luminous Forest glowed all around them. Mushrooms shone like tiny lamps. The moss sparkled beneath Bob’s feet. Somewhere above, night birds sang to one another.

The farther Bob and Leo traveled, the quieter the forest became.

The golden moss grew dim. The bright green leaves faded to dusty silver. Even the night birds fell silent.

Soon, Bob could hear only his own footsteps and the soft hum of Leo’s wheels.

The arrow stopped at the edge of a grove.

Tall willow trees surrounded a dry stream bed. Their long branches hung like curtains. Faint points of light trembled on the ends of their leaves.

“This is the Whispering Willow Grove,” Leo said. “Its light is far below normal.”

Bob listened.

The willows moved in the breeze, but they made no sound.

“They are not whispering,” he said.

Leo scanned the grove. A thin beam of blue light swept across the ground.

“The loss of energy is greatest near the spring.”

They followed the dry stream bed to the largest willow.

Clear water should have bubbled from beneath one of its roots. Instead, the opening was covered by smooth stones, wet leaves, tangled roots, and a thick cushion of moss.

Bob knelt beside it.

“The spring is blocked,” he said. “If the water cannot flow, maybe the forest’s energy cannot flow either.”

“That is a reasonable theory,” Leo replied.

The mossy pile gave a tiny squeak.

Bob froze.

A little pink nose appeared between two roots. Then came a pair of whiskers.

A dormouse was curled in the middle of the blockage. It was fast asleep. Its tiny paws rested beneath its chin, and it looked more comfortable than anyone Bob had ever seen.

Leo lowered his voice.

“Removing the blockage without harming the willow will require care.”

The dormouse gave a sleepy snuffle.

“Waking the dormouse,” Leo added, “may also result in a very grumpy dormouse.”


Chapter Four: A Gentle Repair

Bob studied the stones and roots around the sleeping dormouse.

“We cannot pull everything out at once,” he whispered. “It is like one of my complicated builds. We have to find the piece that is not holding all the other pieces in place.”

He pointed to a flat stone near the edge.

Leo extended a narrow tool from his arm. He slowly eased the stone free. Bob caught it before it could make a sound.

Next, Bob held back a vine while Leo loosened three pebbles. Then Leo supported a thick root while Bob removed a bundle of wet leaves.

They placed every stone in a neat row.

The dormouse stirred.

Bob and Leo became perfectly still.

The little creature opened one eye.

Bob held his breath.

The dormouse yawned, tucked its nose beneath its tail, and went back to sleep.

Bob let out his breath very slowly.

At last, only one curved root remained across the spring.

Leo lifted it a little. Bob carefully picked up the dormouse, along with its cushion of moss, and moved the entire bed into a warm hollow nearby.

The dormouse did not wake.

“Ready?” Bob whispered.

“Ready,” Leo said.

Together, they moved the final root.

Water bubbled out of the ground with a happy gurgle.

It curled around the stones, filled the dry stream bed, and flowed between the willows.

Golden light followed the water.

The glow spread through the earth and climbed the trunks of the ancient trees. One by one, the willow leaves shone green, blue, and silver.

Fireflies lifted from the grass. White night flowers opened beside the stream. Their sweet perfume filled the grove.

High above, thousands of willow leaves began to brush together.

The Whispering Willow Grove was whispering again.

Its song was soft and peaceful. It sounded like distant voices singing a lullaby.

The dormouse opened one eye and watched the sparkling stream. Then it curled up in its new bed and went back to sleep.

Bob smiled.

“I think it approves.”

“Or it is too tired to complain,” Leo said.

His status lights glowed a pleased green.


Chapter Five: The Singing Archway

As the grove filled with light, something new appeared beyond the willows.

Two enormous oak trees stood several steps apart. Their upper branches had grown together to form a tall archway.

Velvet moss covered the bark. Tiny mushrooms glowed around the roots. From the opening came a melody like silver bells carried on a warm breeze.

Bob stared at it.

“Was that there before?”

“No,” Leo said. “The return of the spring appears to have awakened it.”

Bob stepped closer.

“Another secret door. This forest is very good at those.”

Leo sent a beam of blue light through the arch.

“The air beyond it is safe. I detect no dangerous energy and no sign of a disturbance in time.”

Bob looked at him.

“You checked for time travel?”

“After the incident with your old toaster, I now check every unusual doorway.”

“My time machine was almost working.”

“It made toast three minutes before you put in the bread.”

“Exactly.”

The music from the archway grew warmer. It seemed to invite them forward.

Bob took a deep breath.

“We stay together. We look around. If anything feels dangerous, we come straight back.”

“A sensible exploration plan,” Leo said.

They stepped beneath the tangled branches.

There was no flash. There was no sudden rush of wind.

It felt like walking through a curtain of warm, sweet air.

On the other side lay a hidden clearing.

The trees were taller there. Their violet-blue leaves turned the moonlight lavender. Silver flowers hung from their branches, and every flower was shaped like a tiny bell.

The breeze moved through them.

Ting. Ting. Tingle-ting.

At the center of the clearing stood a tall black stone.

It was smooth, silent, and dark as the night sky between the stars.


Chapter Six: Tracks Around the Stone

Bob and Leo approached the black stone.

Lavender moonlight shone on its surface. Swirling lines had been carved from its base to its rounded top. They looked like wind, water, and stars all flowing together.

Leo scanned it.

“The stone resembles obsidian,” he said. “However, its inner structure is unlike any volcanic glass in my records.”

Bob touched it with the tips of his fingers.

The surface was cool. Far inside, he felt a tiny vibration.

“It is humming,” he said.

“It is producing the same low note as the crystal and the singing archway.”

Bob looked down.

Narrow grooves crossed the earth around the stone. They were too straight to be animal trails. Two of them ran side by side.

“These look like wheel tracks,” Bob said. “Something mechanical has been here.”

Nearby, four round marks formed a perfect rectangle in the moss. Whatever had once stood there was gone.

A soft rustle came from the violet trees.

Bob’s heart gave one quick thump.

Leo rolled in front of him. His blue eye focused on the shadows.

A red-gold fox stepped into the clearing.

“Flicker!” Bob cried.

Flicker was an old friend. She trotted over with her white-tipped tail waving behind her. First, she pushed her nose into Bob’s hand. Then she rubbed her cheek against Leo’s metal leg.

After greeting them, Flicker walked straight to the obsidian stone.

She sniffed the carvings and placed one paw in a wheel track. Then she sat beside the four round marks and looked at Bob.

“You know something about this place,” Bob said.

Flicker flicked her ears.

Bob crouched beside her. Silver dust glimmered inside each round mark. The same dust covered the bell-shaped flowers overhead.

“It is pollen,” he said. “The marks, the flowers, and the stone are connected.”

Leo listened to the soft notes ringing through the clearing.

“Perhaps the missing part is not a machine,” he said.

“What else could it be?”

Leo looked up at the silver flowers.

“A song.”


Chapter Seven: The Missing Note

Bob moved carefully around the clearing and tapped each silver flower with one finger.

One flower sang a high note.

Another answered with a low note.

Two smaller flowers chimed together.

Whenever a bell rang, one of the carvings on the obsidian stone glowed for a moment.

Leo recorded every sound. Then he projected four dots of light into the air.

Three dots were bright. One remained dark.

“The stone requires four notes,” Leo said. “We have found only three.”

Flicker gave a soft bark.

She followed one of the old wheel tracks to the edge of the clearing and pushed her nose through a curtain of ivy.

Bob hurried after her.

Behind the leaves, a fallen branch had trapped one silver flower against the ground. Its stem was bent but not broken.

“There you are,” Bob whispered.

He lifted the branch.

The flower slowly rose. Its silver petals opened, and it rang with a clear, warm note.

The fourth dot in Leo’s pattern lit up.

All four round marks beside the stone began to glow.

“I have the complete sequence,” Leo said.

He played the four notes.

The flowers answered him.

Their music traveled around the clearing, bloom by bloom. Soon, every silver bell was ringing in harmony.

Golden light filled the carvings on the obsidian stone. The old wheel tracks glowed like tiny rails.

Bob stood close to Leo. Flicker sat beside them with her ears pointing forward.

Above the stone, the light gathered into a hazy shape.

First, two small wheels appeared.

Then came a metal body. Two arms unfolded from its sides. A round head formed above them, and two bright eyes blinked awake.

The golden light vanished.

A small robot landed on the moss with a surprised beep.

Bob stared.

Leo’s lights flashed green, blue, and gold.

“Unit 734?” Bob and Leo cried together.


Chapter Eight: An Old Friend Comes Home

Unit 734 looked at Bob. Then he looked at Leo and Flicker.

A cheerful burst of beeps came from his speaker.

“Friends located,” he announced. “This result is much better than the other possibilities I calculated.”

Bob ran forward. He stopped just before knocking the little robot over and held up one hand.

Unit 734 gave him a high five.

Clang!

“Where have you been?” Bob asked. “How did you come out of a stone?”

Unit 734 turned toward the glowing carvings.

“I detected a signal while traveling far from the Luminous Forest,” he explained. “The signal contained a location and a repeating musical code. When I played the code, a doorway opened.”

Leo looked at the obsidian stone.

“A doorway through space.”

“Correct,” said Unit 734. “This stone is one end of an ancient transport system. A machine once stood in the four round marks. It helped travelers use the doorway. The machine is gone, but the bell flowers can still activate the stone.”

Bob looked at the old tracks and smiled.

“So we did not find the missing machine.”

“No,” Leo said. “We found another way to make the system work.”

Flicker walked around Unit 734 and sniffed each of his wheels. Then she gave a happy bark.

The light inside the stone became soft and peaceful.

For a moment, a picture appeared on its surface. It showed distant hills beneath unfamiliar stars. Then the image faded, and the stone became dark once more.

Bob could see their reflections in it. A boy, two robots, and a clever fox stood together in the lavender moonlight.

They sat among the bell flowers while Unit 734 told them about his travels. He had crossed red canyons where the wind made the rocks sing. He had visited a lake so still that it reflected the stars during the day. He had even found a village where people made tiny lanterns shaped like moons.

Bob listened until his excitement turned into a warm and sleepy happiness.

At last, Leo checked the position of the moon.

“It is time to return.”

Bob nodded.

Some adventures ended with a race or a great explosion. This adventure had ended with an old friend coming home.

Bob thought that was even better.


Chapter Nine: Safe Until Morning

Flicker led them back through the singing archway.

Unit 734 rolled beside Leo. Their wheels made two quiet trails through the glowing moss. Bob walked between them and tried not to yawn.

He did not succeed.

When they reached the Whispering Willow Grove, the spring was still running brightly beneath the branches.

The dormouse slept in its new hollow.

Bob rebuilt a little wall of stones nearby. This time, he placed the stones around the tiny bed instead of across the water. They would shelter the dormouse from the breeze without blocking the spring.

“A better design,” he whispered.

The willow leaves brushed together overhead, as if they agreed.

At last, the friends returned to the ancient silver tree.

The hidden door opened before Bob could touch it.

Deep inside the tree, the great crystal shone with a steady golden light. The picture of the Whispering Willow Grove was bright again.

A new point of light appeared on the map. It marked the hidden clearing and the obsidian stone.

The light pulsed four times.

Once for Bob.

Once for Leo.

Once for Flicker.

And once for Unit 734.

“The forest has added him to its map,” Bob said.

“Perhaps it is welcoming him home,” Leo replied.

The crystal gave a soft and happy chime.

Outside, the night had grown still.

Flicker curled beneath a fern and wrapped her tail over her nose. Unit 734 lowered his lights and rested near the roots of the silver tree.

Bob sat against the warm trunk. Leo settled beside him, and the robot’s gentle hum slowed to a peaceful rhythm.

“Do you think the obsidian stone leads to other places?” Bob asked.

“Almost certainly,” Leo said.

“Do you think we will visit them one day?”

“Almost certainly.”

Bob smiled and closed his eyes.

Above him, the moon shone through the silver leaves. The spring whispered in the distance. Beyond the hidden archway, the bell flowers chimed so softly that they might have been part of a dream.

The Luminous Forest glowed around its sleeping friends.

It kept every path, every secret, and every promise safe until morning.
